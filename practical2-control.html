2 · Practical 1 model and controller design

Use the averaged gain K̄ and time constant τ̄ from your three Practical 1 voltage step tests and MATLAB validation. The P1 experimental model gives gearbox-output speed in rad/s from motor-terminal volts: Gexp(s) = K̄ / (τ̄s + 1). Practical 2 starts from those results.

Conversion uses the declared supply voltage and the verified motor/output gear ratio from Hardware setup. Use the same gearbox as P1: 4.4:1 or 21.3:1 as applicable.

For the P1 voltage-input/output-rad/s model: Ku = K̄ × N × 60/(2π) × Vs; τ = τ̄. The constant gearbox ratio changes gain, not time constant. “Use converted model” copies only the model fields below; it never changes any PID gains or sends a motor command.
Optional model-based gain helper

Controller model and initial gains

The controller model uses motor-shaft RPM per fractional duty: G(s) = Ku / (τs + 1). Suggestions are calculated only; they never overwrite the controller gains.

Speed PI: Kp = τ/(KuTc), Ki = 1/(KuTc), Kd = 0. Choose the desired time constant Tc from your response requirements. Validate suggested gains with the actual controller limits and sampling before increasing the reference.

3 · Compare P, then add I — simulation only

Use your converted model. First compare three proportional gains. Then keep P fixed and compare integral gains. These ideal continuous-model predictions do not include friction, sampling or actuator saturation; check the duty warning and the separate limited simulator before hardware use.

All simulations use motor RPM and fractional duty, matching the hardware gain fields. No hardware gains are filled automatically and no motor commands are sent. A prediction is not a reviewed hardware setting.

P: T(s) = KKp/(τs + 1 + KKp); τcl = τ/(1 + KKp); ess/r = 1/(1 + KKp). This ideal first-order P response has no overshoot but retains offset.
PI: T(s) = K(Kp s + Ki)/[τs² + (1 + KKp)s + KKi]. The zero matters: inspect the complete response.

Enter your model and gains. No λ or pole choice is needed for this gain-effect comparison.
Three tuning methods — not three hardware experiments

Model-based analysis: explain the P/PI equations and gain effects. Computational tuning: adjust or search simulated gains, then validate a reviewed candidate. Ziegler–Nichols: assess applicability; the ideal delay-free first-order model has neither a positive reaction-curve delay nor a finite positive ultimate gain. Do not invent these values or force physical oscillation.

How results are measured

Overshoot is measured beyond the commanded target, divided by the commanded step size. Rise time is the 10–90% crossing interval. Settling uses a band around the commanded target and requires all later assessment samples to stay inside it for at least two seconds. The final two seconds supply mean absolute error, signed bias and variation. Missing, stale or insufficient data are not a pass. Speed bands use the larger of 2% of the commanded step and two count/window quanta. For 44 counts/motor revolution and a 50 ms window, the floor is ±54.545 motor RPM. At a 1000 RPM target that is ±5.45%, not strict ±2%. Command time is not shifted to the beginning of motion. Any excluded pre-command transition frames are counted; raw CSV data remain unchanged. Values are teaching metrics, not independently certified mechanical accuracy.
