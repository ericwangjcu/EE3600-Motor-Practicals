\sect{0. Purpose}
Use your validated P1 values $\overline K$, $\overline\tau$ and gearbox ratio $N$ for feedback speed control. Choose \textbf{one applicable method}: analytical, Ziegler--Nichols or computational. Simulate, implement on the Pico and assess the measured response. PI is sufficient if it meets the targets. Position control is optional (page 4).

\sect{1. Handover from Practical 1}
Carry forward $G_{P1}(s)=\overline K/(\overline\tau s+1)$, from terminal volts to gearbox-output rad/s. With signed fractional duty $u$ ($0.40=40\%$) and motor-board supply $V_s$, use nominal $V_a\approx V_su$:
\[
G_n(s)=\frac{n_{\mathrm{motor}}(s)}{u(s)}=\frac{\K}{\tau s+1},\qquad
\boxed{\K=\overline K\,N\,\frac{60}{2\pi}V_s,\quad\tau=\overline\tau.}
\]
$\K$ is motor RPM per fractional duty. The webpage's model helper labels this plant gain $K_u$; this sheet uses $\K$ to distinguish it from the Ziegler--Nichols ultimate gain.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Motor-drive change:} with power off, move the motor leads to the driver's \textbf{Motor 1} and connect the supply to the board's power input. Retain the P1 encoder wiring.
\item \textbf{P2 settings:} \ui{Counts / motor revolution} = \textbf{44} (x4-decoded counts); \ui{Gear ratio (motor / output)} = \textbf{21.3} for the demonstrated platform; use the confirmed $N$ for other kits. Do not enter P1's effective \textbf{937 output-shaft counts} as motor-shaft counts.
\end{itemize}
Keep the P1 motor and load; assess driver/supply effects in the new closed-loop results.

\sect{2. Speed Targets and Proportional Baseline}
\sub{A) Required performance}
Use an achievable step $r_0\to r_f$ in motor RPM; compare designs under the same conditions.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{38mm}p{116mm}@{}}
\toprule
Quantity & Target and definition\\
\midrule
Overshoot & $\leq5\%$ of the commanded step magnitude, beyond the final target.\\
Rise time $t_r$ & $\leq0.50$ s; 10--90\% of the commanded change.\\
Settling time $t_s$ & $\leq1.00$ s; remain inside the target band for all later assessment samples, including at least 2 s final dwell.\\
Final tracking error & Last-2-s mean absolute error $\leq\max(0.02|r_f|,2q_n)$ motor RPM. Also report signed bias.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For $C_m$ counts per motor revolution and the 50 ms speed window, $q_n=60/(0.050C_m)$ motor RPM. The settling band is $b_n=\max(0.02|r_f-r_0|,2q_n)$ about $r_f$. With $C_m=44$, the floor is 54.5 motor RPM. Report the displayed band and whether the floor governs it. Insufficient data cannot establish a pass.

\sub{B) Simulate a P-control baseline}
Use unity negative feedback, $e=r-y$, and $u=C(s)e$. For $C(s)=K_p$,
\[
T(s)=\frac{C(s)G_n(s)}{1+C(s)G_n(s)},\qquad
T_P(s)=\frac{\K K_p}{\tau s+1+\K K_p}.
\]
The ideal $\tau_{\mathrm{cl}}=\tau/(1+\K K_p)$ and step-error ratio $e_{ss}/r_f=1/(1+\K K_p)$ show that finite P speeds the response and reduces, but does not remove, offset. Simulate and record a modest gain within the duty limit.

\sect{3. Analytical Method: Initial Speed PI Design}
Choose a desired closed-loop time constant $T_c$. To obtain $T_n(s)=1/(T_cs+1)$ in the ideal model, use
\[
C(s)=K_p+\frac{K_i}{s}=\frac{\tau s+1}{\K T_c s},\qquad
\boxed{K_p=\frac{\tau}{\K T_c},\quad K_i=\frac{1}{\K T_c},\quad K_d=0.}
\]
The controller zero cancels the model's stable pole. This gives $t_r=\ln(9)T_c$, a 2\% settling time $t_s=\ln(50)T_c$, zero nominal overshoot and zero nominal step error. Hence the required times give
\[
T_c\leq\min\!\left(\frac{0.50}{\ln9},\frac{1.00}{\ln50}\right)=0.228\ \mathrm{s}.
\]
For example, $T_c=0.20$ s predicts $t_r=0.439$ s and $t_s=0.782$ s. Insert \emph{your} $\K$ and $\tau$ to obtain initial gains. These are model predictions: finite duty, measurement delay and model mismatch may require a slower design or smaller reference step. Validate the complete response before implementation.

\sect{4. Empirical Method: Ziegler--Nichols}
The ultimate-gain method needs a finite positive proportional gain $K_u$ that produces sustained oscillation with period $T_u$. \textbf{The ideal first-order speed model has no such gain:} its characteristic polynomial $\tau s+1+\K K_p$ remains stable for every $K_p>0$.

If the instructor supplies a suitable higher-order/delayed example or recorded experiment, identify $K_u,T_u$ from it and use the table. Otherwise, explain the limitation and select the analytical or computational route. Do not keep increasing gain to force sustained oscillation on this model or the physical motor.

\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
Controller & $K_p$ & $K_i$ & $K_d$\\
\midrule
P & $0.5K_u$ & $0$ & $0$\\
PI & $0.45K_u$ & $1.2K_p/T_u$ & $0$\\

These parallel gains are initial values; Ziegler--Nichols may be too aggressive for a 5\% overshoot target. The reaction-curve alternative also needs an applicable response with a resolved positive delay $L$; the delay-free first-order model has $L=0$. For $K e^{-Ls}/(Ts+1)$, its PID rule is $K_p=1.2T/(KL)$, including the plant gain.

\sect{5. Computational Method and Simulation Checks}
Use MATLAB/Simulink or a suitable alternative with the converted speed model and unity negative feedback. Start with PI. Use a tuning tool or the lecture's parameter-search approach, state the search range/settings, and assess every candidate against the Section 2 targets.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Controller convention:} use parallel gains. If software reports $K_p(1+1/(T_is)+T_ds)$, convert $K_i=K_p/T_i$ and $K_d=K_pT_d$. PI uses $K_d=0$.
\item \textbf{If $K_d>0$:} match the Pico with PI on error minus filtered D on measured output, $T_f=0.020$ s (derivative setpoint weight zero).
\item Check reference tracking and applied duty, including output saturation. The control interval is 10 ms and the speed window is 50 ms. Do not add the same measurement lag twice if it was already included in the fitted P1 model.
\item When using \texttt{stepinfo}, assess against the commanded step/final target, including any tracking offset; do not accept a response that merely settles about the wrong speed.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Whichever route you choose,} save the initial gains and simulation, then use page 3 to test and refine the controller. You do not need to complete all three tuning methods on hardware.

\sect{8. Optional Extension: Position Control}
Complete the required speed task first. This extension controls the gearbox-output angle, in degrees relative to a stopped zero. Use the same tuning, simulation and measured-validation approach, with the position model and gains below.

\sub{A) Obtain the position model from the speed model}
Because position integrates speed,
\[
G_\theta(s)=\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{out}}(s)}{u(s)}=\frac{A}{s(\tau s+1)},\qquad
A=\frac{6\K}{N}=\frac{180}{\pi}V_s\overline K.
\]
This is output degrees per fractional duty; do not multiply by $N$ again. With P control,
\[
T_\theta(s)=\frac{A K_p}{\tau s^2+s+A K_p},\qquad
\omega_n^2=\frac{A K_p}{\tau},\qquad 2\zeta\omega_n=\frac{1}{\tau}.
\]
Thus \textbf{P cannot set damping and natural frequency independently}. The plant integrator gives zero nominal step-position error in a stable ideal loop; integral control is not automatically required. Test P first or proceed to PD/computational design.

\sub{B) Design PD from the desired response}
Keep the lecture's optional \textbf{simulation} goals: overshoot $\leq5\%$, $0.25\leq t_s\leq0.80$ s and zero nominal steady-state error. Choose damping and a settling-time aim; for the standard second-order approximation,
\[
\zeta\geq0.70\quad\hbox{for a 5\% overshoot limit},\qquad
\omega_n\approx\frac{4}{\zeta t_s}.
\]
For derivative on measurement, the ideal unfiltered controller is $u=K_p(r-\theta)-K_d\dot\theta$, giving
\[
T_\theta(s)=\frac{A K_p}{\tau s^2+(1+A K_d)s+A K_p},\qquad
\boxed{K_p=\frac{\tau\omega_n^2}{A},\quad
K_d=\frac{2\zeta\omega_n\tau-1}{A},\quad K_i=0.}
\]
If $K_d<0$, revisit the chosen response or test P; do not enter negative derivative gain. Validate the complete response with the 10 ms control interval, duty limits and filtered D on measurement ($T_f=0.020$ s). PD on error adds a reference-path zero, so its step response is different.

The ideal position characteristic $\tau s^2+s+A K_p$ is stable for every $K_p>0$ and has no finite positive Ziegler--Nichols ultimate gain. Its integrator also rules out the usual open-loop reaction-curve rule. Use analytical/computational design unless a suitable different example is supplied.
