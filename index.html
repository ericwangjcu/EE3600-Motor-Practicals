Why this practical matters for Assignment 1

From the measured speed step response you will first identify an experimental first-order transfer function. You will then determine the physical parameters R, Ke, Kt, D and J. The first-order model is useful for checking the measured response, but Assignment 1 needs the physical parameters because additional gearbox, wheel, vehicle inertia, damping and load terms must be reflected back to the motor.

Keep your final parameter table and CSV files. You will use these values directly in Assignment 1.

MATLAB is used in Practical 1 for a short validation exercise. After identifying the motor parameters, you will compare the measured step response with both the experimental first-order model and the physical motor model. The full drivetrain and Simulink modelling are carried forward to Assignment 1.

Before you connect power — check your motor platform

Different practical platforms may use different motors. Some motors are rated for approximately 6 V and some for approximately 12 V. The gearbox ratio and rated output speed may also differ. Do not assume that every platform is identical.

If you cannot confirm the motor rated voltage, rated output speed or gearbox ratio, stop and ask the tutor before running the motor. The value 21.6 is the ratio used by the demonstrated platform only.

Steady-state tests: after confirming the rated voltage, take three operating points at approximately 70%, 80% and 90% of the rated voltage (or the values specified by the tutor). Record the actual measured motor-terminal voltage at each point.

Motor power wiring

Use the bench power supply to drive the motor in Practical 1. Do not power the motor from a Pico GPIO pin.

On the motor platform shown in class, the two motor winding leads are the red and white wires. With the supply switched off, connect the red winding lead to the positive motor-supply terminal and the white winding lead to the negative terminal for the normal test direction. If the motor rotates in the opposite direction, switch the supply off before swapping the two motor leads. Do not connect the encoder wires to the power supply.

  • Set a sensible current limit before starting.
  • Choose an applied voltage that does not exceed the confirmed motor rating.
  • Measure and record the actual motor terminal voltage; do not rely only on a nominal setting.
  • If the motor specification or wiring is unclear, ask the tutor before applying power.

Waiting for encoder

The encoder sign only determines which rotation direction is reported as positive speed. It does not change the actual direction of the motor.

  1. Connect the motor in the normal test direction and run it at a low safe voltage.
  2. Check the displayed gearbox-output speed.
  3. If the speed is positive, leave the encoder sign as it is.
  4. If the speed is negative while the motor is rotating in the intended forward direction, change the encoder sign from −1 to +1, or from +1 to −1, then click Apply encoder settings.
Use a positive speed for the Practical 1 calculations. Change the encoder sign to correct the measurement sign rather than reversing the motor wiring just to make the displayed speed positive.

Part 1 — Measure the first-order step response

  1. With the motor stopped, start recording.
  2. Apply the selected safe motor voltage as a step input.
  3. Allow the motor to reach a steady speed, then stop recording.
  4. Repeat the same step-response test three times using the same applied voltage.
  5. For each run, determine the steady output speed and the time constant: τ1, τ2, τ3.
  6. Use the average time constant τ̄ = (τ1 + τ2 + τ3)/3 for the physical parameter calculation.
ωout(τ) = 0.632 ωout,ss
Important: the time constant τ is measured directly from the output-speed response. Do not multiply τ by the gearbox ratio. A constant gear ratio changes the speed magnitude but does not change the time constant.

For each run, convert the steady output speed to rad/s and calculate the steady-state gain. Use the average gain and average time constant for the experimental first-order model:

Ki = ωout,ss,i / Vi      K̄ = (K1+K2+K3)/3
Gexp(s) = K̄ / (τ̄s + 1)

Part 2 — Physical parameters for Assignment 1

2A. Winding resistance

With power removed, measure the winding resistance at five different rotor positions and use the average value R̄. This reduces variation caused by the brushes and commutator.

2B. Three steady-state operating points

Use the three steady-state measurements at approximately 70%, 80% and 90% of rated voltage. The Practical 1 webpage displays gearbox-output speed. For each point, convert it to the real motor-shaft speed using the confirmed gearbox ratio:

nm,i = N nout,i      ωm,i = 2πnm,i/60

For each operating point calculate:

Ke,i = (Vi − R̄Ii) / ωm,i

Then average the three values:

K̄e = (Ke,1 + Ke,2 + Ke,3)/3
Kt ≈ K̄e   (SI units)

Using this Kt, calculate an effective damping value at each of the same three operating points:

Di = KtIi / ωm,i
D̄ = (D1 + D2 + D3)/3

2C. Equivalent inertia

Use the average time constant from the three step-response tests together with the averaged physical parameters:

J = τ̄ ( D̄ + KtK̄e/R̄ )
This is why Practical 1 does more than fit K and τ. Assignment 1 needs R, Ke, Kt, D and J so that the additional drivetrain inertia, damping and loads can be reflected back to the motor correctly.

Part 3 — MATLAB validation

Use MATLAB to compare the measured Practical 1 response with the two transfer-function models you have identified. Simulink is not required for this practical.

  1. Import one of your step-response CSV files. Set t = 0 at the start of the voltage step/speed rise.
  2. Convert the measured gearbox-output speed to rad/s, or convert the simulated responses to rpm before plotting. Keep the units consistent.
  3. Create the experimental first-order model using K̄ and τ̄.
  4. Create the physical motor + built-in gearbox model using R̄, K̄e, Kt, D̄, J and N.
  5. Use the actual measured step voltage from the selected run and simulate both models.
  6. Plot the measured response, experimental first-order model and physical model on the same graph.
  7. Briefly comment on how closely the two models match the real motor response.
Gexp(s) = K̄ / (τ̄s + 1)
Gphys(s) = (Kt/N) / [R̄Js + (R̄D̄ + KtK̄e)]
G_exp  = tf(Kbar, [taubar 1]);
G_phys = tf(Kt/N, [Rbar*J, Rbar*Dbar + Kt*Kebar]);

u = Vstep*ones(size(t));
y_exp  = lsim(G_exp,  u, t);
y_phys = lsim(G_phys, u, t);

The transfer functions above give output speed in rad/s. Convert to rpm with rpm = ω × 60/(2π) if you want to compare directly with the webpage CSV.

Link to Assignment 1: Practical 1 validates the real motor model. In Assignment 1 you will take the physical parameters into the full drivetrain model, reflect the additional inertia/damping back to the motor and use MATLAB/Simulink for the vehicle/control-system analysis.

Part 4 — Optional added-inertia validation

If the supplied disc is used, calculate its output-shaft inertia and reflect it to the motor shaft:

Jdisc,out = ½mr²      Jdisc,m = Jdisc,out/N²

Predict the new time constant before repeating the test:

τnew = (J + Jdisc,m) / ( D̄ + KtK̄e/R̄ )

Compare the prediction with the measured response. The added inertia should mainly slow the transient response; it should not be treated as a known steady load torque.
